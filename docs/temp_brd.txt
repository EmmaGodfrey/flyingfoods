1. Executive Summary
This Business Requirements Document defines a new Restaurant and Inventory Management System for
Flying Foods. The system takes over every workflow that happens after the existing POS produces a
receipt. The POS continues to handle cashiering, card payment, receipt printing, and sales reporting
(sales per cashier, hourly breakdowns, payment types). The new system handles kitchen fulfillment,
waiter service, inventory at three locations (Restaurant Stores, Restaurant Kitchen, in-flight Unit),
procurement (Budget then PO then Supplier then GRN then Invoice), wastage and stock-take, inter-unit
transfers, and budget reporting.
The system integrates outward to two external systems: the existing POS (inbound sale events) and
Pastel (outbound stock movements for accounting). It also emails Purchase Orders to suppliers directly
while keeping a complete in-system audit log of every PO sent.

1.1 Business Drivers
   •   Eliminate the paper-and-Excel inventory workflow and replace it with a single source of truth.
   •   Get the kitchen, the stores, the procurement function, and the accounting system speaking the
       same language in real time.
   •   Make supplier engagement traceable: every PO recorded, every GRN matched, every invoice
       reconciled.
   •   Surface real consumption (recipe-driven deductions plus wastage and adjustments) so Budget vs
       Actual reflects reality.
   •   Stop duplicating work between operations and accounting by syncing stock movements to
       Pastel automatically.

1.2 Automation Principles
The system replaces every physical handover, paper form, and parallel spreadsheet with an automated
digital flow. The principles below govern the design.
     • No manual receipt entry. The POS pushes (or our system pulls) the sale automatically and the
          kitchen ticket appears on the Kitchen Display the moment the sale lands.
     • No paper GRN, PO or issue notes. Every procurement and stock document lives in the system.
     • No parallel spreadsheets. The system is the single source of truth for inventory, procurement,
          and audit history; Pastel receives a synchronised feed of stock movements.
     • No manual stock deduction for sales. The system deducts ingredients automatically using the
          active recipe version.
     • No handwritten issue or transfer notes. Issues and transfers are created in the system at the
          point of action.
     • Human action is reserved for decisions (approvals, exception handling, marking out-of-stock,
          marking ready/served), not for data re-entry.

4. Business Process Overview
This section describes the core business processes the system supports. Each is illustrated with a flow
diagram. Detailed functional requirements appear in Section 6 and user stories in Section 7.

4.1 Process A: Sale Capture → Kitchen Fulfillment → Service
A sale is recorded in the POS and the sale event arrives in the new system (push or pull, per Section 3.1).
The system immediately routes an order ticket to the Kitchen Display and deducts the recipe ingredients
from the restaurant stores Opening Balance, syncing the deduction to Pastel. The Chef updates the
status to In Preparation, prepares the order, and logs any extra ingredient usage (e.g. a dropped egg)
before marking the order Ready. The order then appears on the Waiter view; the Waiter delivers it and
marks it Served. No physical handover and no manual data entry between any of these steps.

                        Figure 4. Process A: Sale capture, kitchen fulfillment & service


4.2 Process B: Procurement: Budget → PO → Supplier → GRN
A requester (Unit, Restaurant, or Stores) submits a Purchase Budget listing the items and quantities
needed (e.g. "50 tomatoes"). The Manager reviews and approves on screen. Once approved, the
Receiving Officer creates a Purchase Order against the approved budget, selecting one or more
suppliers. The system emails the PO to each supplier and records the send. When the goods arrive, the
Receiving Officer enters the GRN against the PO, capturing actual quantity received, unit cost, and any
variance. The system increases the Opening Balance at Restaurant Stores, syncs the movement to

Pastel, and updates Budget vs Actual. The supplier invoice is later matched against the PO and GRN in a
three-way match before payment is approved.

4.3 Process C: Inventory Issuing & Inter-Unit Transfers

The Restaurant Issuer creates issue requests against the restaurant stores every day. The Unit Issuer
creates issue requests every Tuesday and Thursday. Each issue is recorded in the system, which deducts
from central stores Opening Balance and adds to the destination location (Restaurant Kitchen or Unit).
The Restaurant and Unit can also transfer stock between each other; transfers above a configurable
threshold require Manager approval. Every movement is mirrored to Pastel per the integration option
chosen.




                         Figure 6. Process C: Inventory issuing & inter-unit transfers


4.4 Process D: Wastage, Breakage & Stock-Take
Recipe-driven auto-deduction handles the normal case, but real operations produce variances. Three
event types feed the wastage ledger: in-the-moment extra usage during preparation (logged by Chef or
Storekeeper against the active order); breakage or spoilage events (logged with a reason code); and
periodic stock-take variances (physical count vs system on-hand). Adjustments above a configurable
monetary threshold require Manager approval before posting to stock. All adjustments are mirrored to
Pastel and feed into the Wastage Report and Budget vs Actual so real consumption is visible.

4.5 Process E: Menu & Recipe Management
Menus and recipes change. The system uses versioned menu items and recipes with effective-from
dates: editing a recipe creates a new version, never overwrites the previous one. Historical orders
always reference the recipe version that was active when the order was placed, so Budget vs Actual and
cost reports remain accurate over time. A Draft → Review → Published workflow lets the Administrator
prepare changes (e.g. a seasonal menu) ahead of an effective date without affecting live service.

5. Assumptions, Constraints & Dependencies
5.1 Assumptions
   •   The existing POS will continue to be the system of record for cashiering, card payment, receipt
       printing, and sales-side reporting (sales per cashier, hourly, payment type).
   •   The POS can either push sale events or expose data we can pull (final integration approach
       pending vendor confirmation).
   •   Pastel exposes an API or import format suitable for posting inventory movements.
   •   Every menu item has a defined recipe so ingredient deduction from stock is automatic.
   •   The Restaurant operates a single central stores location; the Unit operates its own sub-
       inventory.
   •   Each role has access to a workstation, tablet, or shared device on the local network.

5.2 Constraints
   •   Cashiering and payment must remain in the existing POS; the new system must not duplicate
       them.
   •   Unit ordering is restricted to Tuesdays and Thursdays per business policy.
   •   The kitchen and stores areas must remain usable during short internet outages.

5.3 Dependencies
   •   Vendor cooperation from the POS supplier to confirm the integration approach (push API, file
       drop, or pull).
   •   Access to Pastel administrator for API credentials and chart-of-accounts mapping for stock
       movements.
   •   Current product master data, supplier list, and opening balances loaded into the new system at
       go-live.
   •   Defined recipes for every active menu item.

7. User Stories & Acceptance Criteria
Stories follow As a <role>, I want <capability> so that <business value>, with testable Given/When/Then
acceptance criteria.

7.1 Chef Stories
US-CHF-01: Chef
As a Chef, I want see every paid order on the Kitchen Display the moment the POS records it so that I
can start preparing without waiting on anyone to tell me.
Acceptance Criteria
   • When the POS records a sale, the order appears on my Kitchen Display within 5 seconds of
       ingestion.
   • Each order card shows order number, table or counter, items, modifiers, and time since
       ingestion.
   • I never need to receive a paper ticket. The system is the channel.

US-CHF-02: Chef
As a Chef, I want mark an order In Preparation and then Ready so that the Waiter and the counter see
real-time order status.
Acceptance Criteria
   • Tapping Start changes status to In Preparation and records a timestamp.
   • Tapping Ready moves the order to the Waiter view.
   • A Ready order remains visible on my display until the Waiter marks it Served, so I can spot
       delays.

US-CHF-03: Chef
As a Chef, I want log extra ingredient usage against an order when something goes wrong so that
stock-on-hand stays accurate even when reality differs from the recipe.
Acceptance Criteria
   • When I add an extra-usage entry (item, quantity, reason), the system records who logged it.
   • Entries below the threshold post immediately; above the threshold they go to Manager
       approval.
   • I cannot mark the order Ready until a required approval is resolved or I withdraw the entry.

7.2 Waiter Stories
US-WTR-01: Waiter
As a Waiter, I want see all orders that are Ready in one view so that I know what to deliver without
walking back to ask.
Acceptance Criteria
   • My view lists every Ready order with table, items, and time-since-Ready, oldest first.
   • A new Ready order appears within 5 seconds of the Chef marking it Ready.

US-WTR-02: Waiter
As a Waiter, I want mark an order Served once delivered so that the team and Management can see
real service status.
Acceptance Criteria
   • Tapping Served moves the order out of my view and records the timestamp against my user.
   • The Service Time report can now calculate Ingested → InPrep → Ready → Served.

US-WTR-03: Waiter
As a Waiter, I want flag an order Returned with a reason so that the kitchen can correct it and the
issue is captured for review.
Acceptance Criteria
   • Tapping Return and selecting a reason sends the order back to In Preparation.
   • My user and the reason are recorded against the order.

7.3 Storekeeper Stories
US-STK-01: Storekeeper
As a Storekeeper, I want have stock deducted automatically by the system when an order is placed so
that I never manually adjust stock for sales and can focus on stock-take and exceptions.
Acceptance Criteria
   • When a sale reaches the Kitchen Display, the recipe's ingredients are deducted from Restaurant
       Stores with no action from me.
   • A deduction that would go below zero triggers an alert to me.
   • I can review every automatic deduction in an audit log and reverse one with a captured reason.

US-STK-02: Storekeeper
As a Storekeeper, I want see real-time stock-on-hand by item so that I know what is low without
counting manually.
Acceptance Criteria
   • Stock On Hand shows current quantities by item, sorted by category.
   • Items at or below reorder level are highlighted.

US-STK-03: Storekeeper
As a Storekeeper, I want log breakage, spoilage and other wastage events so that physical stock and
system stock stay aligned.
Acceptance Criteria
   • I enter item, quantity, reason code, and optional note.
   • Below threshold posts immediately; above threshold goes to Manager approval.
   • Posted entries appear in the audit log and sync to Pastel.

US-STK-04: Storekeeper
As a Storekeeper, I want perform a periodic stock-take and have variances posted as adjustments so
that the system reflects reality and I can investigate any large variance.

7.4 Receiving Officer Stories
US-RCV-01: Receiving Officer

US-RCV-02: Receiving Officer

   • If the supplier has no email on file, the system records the PO and prompts me to contact them

US-RCV-03: Receiving Officer

US-RCV-04: Receiving Officer

Acceptance Criteria
